# Reply to Antonio Álvarez (Profand)

**Subject:** Re: Chapter 5 — thank you, and what I've changed

Dear Antonio,

Thank you for this, it's the most useful feedback I've had on the chapter so far and it's led to some real changes.

On your first point about the cohort: you're right that Mowi, Thai Union and Profand are structurally very different firms, and the chapter didn't say enough about why I still treat them as comparable. I've added a passage explaining it. In short, all six are scored against the same external list of 70 ESRS requirements rather than against each other, and the measure is simply whether a topic is addressed at all, a question that works for any business model. The result the chapter actually leans on isn't the individual firm figures but the set of requirements all six report, and if anything your point strengthens that: agreement across six very different firms is better evidence of a genuine sector norm than agreement across six similar ones.

On the mapping methodology: this is where your comments had the biggest effect. Setting the method out properly sent me back through the bridge itself, and I refined how it handles GRI disclosures that map into more than one ESRS standard. It now applies the published GRI–EFRAG interoperability index in full, with the earlier version and a change log kept alongside it so the work stays auditable. Profand's coverage moves from 39 to 43 requirements as a result.

On your description of Profand's 2025 report: corrected. Table 1 now reads "CSRD double materiality; GRI supporting" instead of "GRI, with reference", and the text describes it as a CSRD-structured report with the GRI table appended as a supporting crosswalk.

Now your table, which I went through against both the 2024 and 2025 reports.

On E1-1 you were right and I've fixed it. The climate transition plan has no GRI equivalent at all, so it was unreachable for any firm I coded through a GRI index. Rather than leave that gap, I read the reports directly for it and found it in all three — your Net Zero 2050 roadmap on pages 52 and 55–57 among them. E1-1 is now recorded for five of the six firms and feeds through into Chapter 6.

The other seven are a different case, and here I've kept the coding as it is. My method reads each firm's own framework index rather than the full report — it's the only way to apply one consistent rule across six very different documents, and it's also how a customer's due-diligence team reads a report in practice. E1-2, E1-3, E3-1, E4-2, E4-3, E5-1 and E5-3 all reach the ESRS through a single GRI disclosure: GRI 3-3, management of material topics. Profand's correspondence table indexes GRI 301 to 306 and 308, but no GRI 3-3 rows at all. The other two GRI reporters index GRI 3-3 across most of their material topics, which is why they're credited with all seven.

So the difference isn't what Profand does, it's what the correspondence table exposes. I've said this explicitly in the chapter now, and noted that Profand's figure should be read as a floor rather than a ceiling. I haven't recoded those seven from the report body, because reading one firm's full text while coding the others from their indexes would break exactly the comparability your first comment asks me to defend. And to be clear, this is a limitation of my instrument rather than a criticism of your reporting: GRI 3-3 is optional for firms not applying the standards in full, and a correspondence table appended to a CSRD report isn't trying to be a complete index.

And of course I'll name you in the acknowledgements — thank you for being happy to be credited. I'll send you the final version once I submit on 24 August.

Thank you again, sincerely — this was a real investment of your time and the chapter is much better for it.

Warm regards,
Linne
