## D. Substantive challenges — 6 comments, and these are the valuable ones

He works in the sector. Three of these say a claim is overstated.

**#4 (p.13) — "crowded field" of certification is overstated.**
> Within aquaculture there is ASC, GGAP, BAP (plus Organic); within wild catch MSC and RFM, the latter not really known. "With regard to different certifications this is no crowd." It becomes crowded only when you add rating platforms and requirements beyond certification — GGG, GDST — and notes MSC and ASC both work with GDST.

This is a domain expert saying your framing is emotional rather than accurate. The fix is available in his own comment: the crowd isn't certification schemes, it's the *layering* of certification plus rating platforms plus traceability standards.

**#5 (p.13) — chain of custody is not the burden you imply.**
> Most processors already meet BRC or IFS, which demand full traceability and chain of custody. "So then meeting the MSC/ASC chain of custody standard is actually not that difficult and not very cumbersome."

Directly challenges a cost assumption. Worth taking seriously — it touches the build-first argument for traceability.

**#7 (p.14) — licence to operate / licence to sell have always existed.**
> Financial (payment terms), quality (bones, microbiological thresholds), environmental (certifications), ethical (Human Rights — not currently mentioned). In most cases customer requirements are stricter, but not always: microbiological and human-rights requirements can be higher in law.

Two things here: your framing implies novelty he disputes, and he notes **ethical/human-rights requirements are missing** from your list.

**#3 (p.12) — the "arrives as a shock" claim.**
> "Is this based on empathy from the outside? Or is there a considerable dataset to confirm? How does this shock materialise? Resources? Costs? Consulting agencies — wrong ones? Sunk costs?"

Asking for evidence or a softer claim.

**#8 (p.20) — "neither one alone is manageable".**
> "Why does this conclusion come from?"

Unsupported assertion in the figure.

**#20 and #21 (p.22) — how do you know the six are the most advanced?**
> "How do you know / did you measure that these 6 reporters are most advanced? And are they nonetheless laggards?" — and "does 'the full rationale' answer why these are the most advanced reporters?"

**This is the same question Antonio raised**, from a different angle. Antonio asked why structurally different firms are comparable; Oliver asks how you established "most advanced" at all — and pointedly, whether even they are behind.
