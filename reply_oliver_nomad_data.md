Dear Oliver,

Here's the year-on-year analysis you asked about — summary attached.

Nomad Foods' reporting covers **53 of the 70 topical ESRS disclosure requirements in 2024 and 52 in 2025**. Fifty-two are reported in both years, one falls away, none are added. Full coverage on value-chain workers and on consumers and end-users in both years.

**On method, since it affects how to read that.** I didn't read the reports in full. Doing that across six documents of very different length and structure, on my own and without a fixed protocol, would have produced differences that record how carefully I read rather than what each firm reports. So I coded from each firm's own framework index — the ESRS index where a firm publishes one, the GRI content index where it doesn't — bridged onto the ESRS structure using the GRI–EFRAG interoperability index. It measures breadth, not depth or quality, and it isn't an assessment of ESRS compliance.

**The one change is E1-9, anticipated financial effects from climate risk — and it's an indexing change rather than a disclosure one.** E1-9 is reached through GRI 201-2, financial implications of climate change. Your 2024 index carries 201-2 and points it at the annual report and TCFD statement. Your 2025 index carries 201-1 but not 201-2 — while the TCFD statement itself is still published and linked from the report on pages 35, 61 and 64.

So the content appears to still be there; it's no longer mapped to a GRI disclosure the bridge can reach. That may be worth knowing: a reader working from your GRI index alone won't find the TCFD material credited against the ESRS climate requirements, and increasingly that reader is a customer's due-diligence system rather than a person.

Happy to send the full requirement-by-requirement workbook if useful — it shows which GRI disclosure and which page each of the 52 was coded from. And if anything looks wrong, I'd much rather hear it before I submit on 24 August.

Thank you again for the time you've put into this.

Warm regards,
Linne
