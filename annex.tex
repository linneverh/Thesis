\subsection*{A.1 - Glossary of the seafood sustainability disclosure ecosystem}

Frameworks, regulations, certifications and standards - Layer 1 (demand) and Layer 2 (enablement).

Snapshot: June 2026. Regulatory references and statuses current as of this date; verify against primary sources before final submission.

How to use this glossary

This glossary catalogues every instrument in the seafood sustainability landscape mapped in this thesis. It works both as an orientation read - what each actor in the ecosystem actually is and who runs it - and as a reference appendix to the coverage matrix. Entries are grouped into two layers, mirroring the demand analysis in the methodology.

Dual-role instruments. A few sit on the boundary - TCFD/TNFD are frameworks that also shape investor asks; GDST is a data standard buyers may require; the EU Taxonomy informs financiers. Each is placed by its primary role, with the dual nature noted in its entry.

Each entry records, in a fixed metadata block: the organisation behind the instrument, its legal reference or founding year, its status, its stakeholder source and whether it is an asker or enabler, followed by its objective, its role in the seafood ecosystem, and - where relevant - an evidence note and an official source link.

Reading the flags. Following the project's evidence convention: unmarked statements are verified facts; [LIKELY] marks high-confidence inference and [ASSUMPTION] marks an inferred or uncertain detail. A Status flag highlights instruments whose scope, timing or legal standing was actively changing as of June 2026 - chiefly the EU 'Omnibus' simplification (CSRD, CSDDD, ESRS), the postponed EUDR timetable, the voluntary status of the VSME, and the future application dates of the Forced Labour Regulation and EmpCo.

\subsubsection*{Tier 0 - Foundation (food integrity)}

This entire band sits outside the necessity/domain grid used elsewhere in
this glossary and in Appendix~A.2: it is the non-negotiable food-safety and
food-law floor every EU seafood processor clears before sustainability
strategy begins, in scope of the firm but out of scope of the
sustainability-disclosure analysis and its 49-instrument count.

\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{General Food Law - General principles and requirements of food law}\\
\emph{Objective.} Lays down general food-safety principles, the 'one-step-back / one-step-forward' traceability duty, and the Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed (RASFF).
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The baseline food-safety and traceability regime; the top of the food-integrity floor described above.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Hygiene of Foodstuffs - Regulation (EC) 852/2004}\\
\emph{Objective.} General hygiene rules for food businesses.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Specific Hygiene Rules, Animal Origin - Regulation (EC) 853/2004}\\
\emph{Objective.} Specific hygiene rules for products of animal origin, including fishery products.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Food Information to Consumers - Regulation (EU) 1169/2011}\\
\emph{Objective.} Mandatory food labelling and consumer information requirements (ingredients, allergens, provenance) -- distinct from the sustainability claims EmpCo regulates.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Food Contact Materials - Regulation (EC) 1935/2004}\\
\emph{Objective.} Safety requirements for materials in contact with food -- a packaging-material safety floor, distinct from PPWR's environmental packaging requirements.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Microbiological Criteria - Regulation (EC) 2073/2005}\\
\emph{Objective.} Sets microbiological safety limits for foodstuffs.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Maximum Contaminant Levels - Regulation (EC) 1881/2006}\\
\emph{Objective.} Sets maximum permitted levels of contaminants in food, relevant to seafood contaminant screening (e.g. heavy metals).
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Pesticide Residues - Regulation (EC) 396/2005}\\
\emph{Objective.} Sets maximum residue levels of pesticides in food and feed, relevant chiefly to aquaculture feed inputs.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{REACH - Regulation (EC) 1907/2006}\\
\emph{Objective.} Registration, evaluation and authorisation of chemical substances -- governs chemical inputs (processing aids, cleaning agents) rather than sustainability disclosure.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Official Controls Regulation - Regulation (EU) 2017/625}\\
\emph{Objective.} The framework for official food-safety controls and enforcement across the EU -- the enforcement mechanism behind the food-integrity floor above.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{IFS Food - International Featured Standards - Food}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies food-safety and quality-management systems for processors supplying (mainly continental-European) retail.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Effectively non-negotiable for retail listing; part of the food-integrity floor rather than the sustainability-disclosure system, though commercially it functions much like a Tier-2 market licence.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{BRCGS - Brand Reputation Compliance Global Standards}\\
\emph{Objective.} Global food-safety and quality certification widely required by UK and international retailers.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A near-substitute for IFS Food with the same market-licence function; buyer choice between them is largely geographic.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{FSSC 22000 - Food Safety System Certification 22000}\\
\emph{Objective.} Food-safety system certification combining ISO 22000 with sector-specific prerequisite programmes.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The third common GFSI-benchmarked option; interchangeable with IFS/BRCGS in most buyer specifications.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GFSI - Global Food Safety Initiative}\\
\emph{Objective.} Benchmarks food-safety schemes (IFS, BRCGS, FSSC 22000) for mutual recognition - 'certified once, accepted everywhere'.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The reason IFS, BRCGS and FSSC behave as near-substitutes: buyers accept any GFSI-benchmarked scheme, so they should not be triple-counted as independent demands.

\subsubsection*{Layer 1 - Demand-side instruments (disclosure askers)}
\paragraph*{1A · EU horizontal regulation - disclosure, due diligence \& finance}~\\
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{CSRD - Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive}\\
\emph{Objective.} Expands and standardises mandatory corporate sustainability reporting across the EU, requiring in-scope undertakings to report under the ESRS on a double-materiality basis (impacts on people and planet, and sustainability matters financially material to the firm).
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Sets the scope boundary that decides whether a seafood processor must report at all. Most 50--250-employee processors fall below its thresholds and are reached only as value-chain partners of larger in-scope customers.
\emph{Status flag.} Scope and timing are being narrowed by the 2025 'Omnibus I' simplification package (raising thresholds and delaying waves). Treat the in-scope population as a moving target and verify the consolidated text before citing thresholds.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ESRS - European Sustainability Reporting Standards}\\
\emph{Objective.} The detailed disclosure standards that operationalise the CSRD: two cross-cutting standards (ESRS 1--2) plus ten topical standards (E1--E5, S1--S4, G1), all built on double materiality and a defined set of Disclosure Requirements (DRs).
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The DR-level ruler against which this thesis maps all cohort and SME disclosure; it defines the topical Disclosure Requirements that the coverage matrix scores frameworks against.
\emph{Status flag.} A simplified ESRS set (fewer datapoints) is under EFRAG revision following the Omnibus mandate - the exact DR/datapoint counts may change.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{VSME - Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs}\\
\emph{Objective.} A proportionate voluntary standard for SMEs outside CSRD scope, structured as a Basic Module and a Comprehensive Module, designed to cut the reporting load from roughly a thousand ESRS datapoints to around a hundred and to answer the most common value-chain and lender information requests.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The realistic regulatory reference point - and candidate 'home base' - for non-CSRD seafood SMEs; built specifically so a small supplier can respond to a large customer's ESRS-driven data request once rather than per buyer.
\emph{Status flag.} It is a Commission Recommendation, not a Delegated Act - adoption is encouraged but voluntary as of June 2026. Do not describe it as legally binding.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{CSDDD - Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive}\\
\emph{Objective.} Requires large companies to conduct human-rights and environmental due diligence across their chains of activities - identifying, preventing, mitigating and accounting for adverse impacts. It is a conduct/due-diligence directive rather than a reporting standard.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Drives value-chain due-diligence expectations (e.g. forced labour in fishing fleets) that cascade contractually onto SME suppliers even when those SMEs are not themselves in scope.
\emph{Status flag.} Scope, timing and the civil-liability regime are being scaled back under the 2025 Omnibus package; the first-wave application date has moved. Verify the consolidated text.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{EU Taxonomy - Regulation on the establishment of a framework to facilitate sustainable investment}\\
\emph{Objective.} A classification system defining environmentally sustainable economic activities against six environmental objectives, using technical screening criteria, 'do-no-significant-harm' tests and minimum social safeguards.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Shapes how banks and investors assess 'green' exposure; reaches seafood SMEs mainly through lender and investor alignment questions rather than as a direct obligation.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{SFDR - Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Requires financial market participants and advisers to disclose how they integrate sustainability risks and to report principal adverse impacts (PAI) of their investments.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Indirect pressure on the firm: SFDR-bound funds and banks pass PAI data requests down to investee and borrower seafood companies.
\paragraph*{1B · EU sector \& operational regulation - seafood, food \& labour}~\\
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{CFP - Common Fisheries Policy}\\
\emph{Objective.} The overarching EU framework for managing fishing fleets and conserving stocks - fishing-opportunity setting (TACs/quotas), fleet-capacity ceilings, the landing obligation and a maximum-sustainable-yield (MSY) exploitation objective.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Tier-1 legal licence to operate for wild-capture sourcing; it binds Member States, and processor-facing obligations flow downstream through the Control Regulation, IUU and CMO rather than from the CFP itself.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{IUU Regulation - Illegal, Unreported and Unregulated Fishing Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Prevents, deters and eliminates illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, chiefly through a catch-certificate scheme: imported wild-capture products must carry a catch certificate validated by the flag state.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Anchor of the external traceability corridor - a validated catch certificate is a Tier-1 precondition for placing imported wild fish on the EU market, complementary to (not a subset of) the internal Control Regulation.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Control Regulation (recast) - Fisheries Control Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Modernises EU fisheries control and enforcement - including full-chain digital traceability, mandatory electronic catch documentation and tighter monitoring of the EU fleet and the EU market.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Governs internal EU-fleet control and EU-market traceability (the sibling to IUU's external import scheme); pushes lot-level digital traceability that feeds the traceability-capability corridor.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{CMO Regulation - Common Market Organisation for fishery \& aquaculture products}\\
\emph{Objective.} Organises the EU market for fishery and aquaculture products, including common marketing standards and mandatory consumer information - commercial and scientific species name, production method, catch/production area and gear category.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Binds the processor at the point of sale; its mandatory origin and method labelling overlap directly with chain-of-custody and consumer-information disclosures.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Fisheries Labour Directive - Implementation of the ILO Work in Fishing Convention (C188)}\\
\emph{Objective.} Sets minimum working and living conditions for fishers aboard vessels (contracts, hours/rest, repatriation, accommodation, medical care).
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Binds vessel owners rather than the processor; reaches the firm as value-chain content via buyer SMETA audits and ESRS S2, and as a baseline for forced-labour scrutiny.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{EUDR - EU Deforestation Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Prohibits placing on (or exporting from) the EU market certain commodities linked to deforestation - cattle, soy, palm oil, wood, cocoa, coffee, rubber - without due-diligence statements backed by geolocation evidence.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Relevant to aquaculture feed sourcing (soy and palm derivatives) and to paper/board packaging, and to natural-ecosystem-conversion topics.
\emph{Status flag.} Application was postponed by 12 months: large/medium operators from 30 Dec 2025 and micro/small operators from 30 June 2026 - confirm the current date for your size class.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{PPWR - Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Replaces the 1994 Packaging Directive with directly-applicable rules on packaging minimisation, recyclability, recycled-content targets and labelling for all packaging placed on the EU market.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Touches resource-use and waste (E5) plus the packaging data that retailers increasingly request; binds the processor as the entity placing packaged product on the market.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{EmpCo - Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive}\\
\emph{Objective.} Amends the Unfair Commercial Practices and Consumer Rights Directives to ban generic, unsubstantiated green claims ('eco-friendly', 'climate neutral') and to regulate sustainability labels.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Constrains 'sustainable' and eco claims on seafood packaging and raises the evidentiary bar for certification-based marketing.
\emph{Status flag.} It is the consumer-law half of the EU greenwashing agenda; the companion Green Claims Directive (substantiation rules) was still in negotiation as of mid-2026 - do not conflate the two.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Forced Labour Regulation - Prohibition of products made with forced labour on the Union market}\\
\emph{Objective.} Prohibits placing or making available on the EU market - or exporting - any product made with forced labour at any stage of its production, with investigation and enforcement powers for authorities.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Adds enforcement teeth to forced-labour risk in fishing and processing supply chains; documented due diligence reduces investigation exposure and reinforces S2 value-chain work.
\emph{Status flag.} Applies from 14 Dec 2027 - it is a future obligation, not yet enforceable. State the date when citing it.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{OSH Framework Directive - Safety and health of workers at work}\\
\emph{Objective.} Establishes the employer's general duty to ensure workers' health and safety - risk assessment, prevention principles, information and training.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Legal baseline behind own-workforce health-and-safety (S1) disclosure; especially salient for cutting lines, machinery and cold stores in seafood processing.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Working Time Directive - Organisation of working time}\\
\emph{Objective.} Sets minimum requirements for working hours, rest periods, breaks, paid annual leave and night work.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Underpins the working-conditions elements of own-workforce (S1) disclosure.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{VMP Regulation - Veterinary Medicinal Products Regulation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Governs authorisation and use of veterinary medicines, including measures to curb antimicrobial use and resistance (e.g. restrictions on prophylactic and metaphylactic antibiotics).
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The regulatory anchor for antimicrobial use in aquaculture, alongside the Farm to Fork antimicrobial-reduction target - folded into the animal health \& welfare structural-gap topic in the matrix, one of four such topics.
\paragraph*{1C · Customer-driven standards \& audits}~\\
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{MSC - Marine Stewardship Council}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies wild-capture fisheries against a science-based sustainability standard (stock status, ecosystem impact, management) and tracks certified product through the supply chain via a Chain-of-Custody standard, assessed by accredited third-party conformity-assessment bodies.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A de-facto market licence for many wild-capture retail listings; its Chain of Custody sits squarely on the traceability corridor. Wild-caught scope only.
\emph{Evidence.} Peer-reviewed work links certification to healthier stock indicators - Gutiérrez et al. (2012, PLoS ONE) found the ecolabel conveys reliable stock-health information - though effect-size and selection-bias debates persist (see 'Shifting focus: the impacts of sustainable seafood certification', PLoS ONE). Use as associative, not causal, evidence.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ASC - Aquaculture Stewardship Council}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies responsibly farmed seafood against species-specific environmental and social standards (water quality, feed, disease/antimicrobials, labour), with a chain-of-custody linked to the MSC CoC programme.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Market expectation for farmed seafood such as salmon, shrimp and pangasius; covers welfare, antimicrobials, water and feed. Aquaculture scope only.
\emph{Evidence.} ASC and MSC merged their chain-of-custody programme; for farmed-stock outcome evidence the literature is thinner and more recent than for wild MSC - flag any effectiveness claim as emerging. [LIKELY]
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{BAP - Best Aquaculture Practices}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies aquaculture facilities across the chain - hatchery, farm, feed mill and processing plant - against environmental, social, food-safety and animal-welfare criteria, using a 'star' rating for chain completeness.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Alternative or complement to ASC and the only major scheme certifying feed mills directly; strong in North American buyer requirements. Aquaculture scope.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GLOBALG.A.P. - Good Aquaculture Practice (Aquaculture standard)}\\
\emph{Objective.} Business-to-business good-practice certification covering food safety, environmental management and worker welfare at the production stage, with an aquaculture-specific module.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A common retailer prerequisite, particularly for farmed produce; often the production-stage layer beneath a retailer's social and food-safety asks. Aquaculture scope (for the module in view).
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Friend of the Sea - Friend of the Sea}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies wild and farmed seafood (and other products) against sustainability criteria, with an ecolabel.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} An additional ecolabel option with lower retail penetration than MSC/ASC but present in the landscape; covers both production systems.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Sedex / SMETA - Supplier Ethical Data Exchange / Sedex Members Ethical Trade Audit}\\
\emph{Objective.} Shares ethical-trade data among members and provides a standard social-audit methodology (SMETA) covering labour, health \& safety, environment and business ethics.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A common retailer-required social audit; feeds S1/S2 worker and H\&S topics and sits on the supplier-engagement corridor.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{EcoVadis - EcoVadis}\\
\emph{Objective.} Scores suppliers on environment, labour \& human rights, ethics and sustainable procurement through a documentation-based assessment yielding a medal/percentile scorecard.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Frequently requested by large retail and B2B buyers; its broad multi-topic scope makes it a strong hotspot driver in the demand matrix.
\paragraph*{1D · Investor-driven frameworks}~\\
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{CDP - CDP (formerly Carbon Disclosure Project)}\\
\emph{Objective.} Runs a global environmental disclosure system (climate, water security, forests) through scored questionnaires (A--D) used by investors and large buyers to request standardised data.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A common investor and large-customer climate \& water questionnaire; strong overlap with GHG and water topics, and a frequent conduit by which buyer demand reaches the SME.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{SBTi - Science Based Targets initiative}\\
\emph{Objective.} Defines and independently validates corporate emissions-reduction targets aligned with climate science (1.5\textdegree{}C pathways), including FLAG (Forest, Land and Agriculture) guidance relevant to feed and protein.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Signals a proactive climate posture; validated targets feed CDP and ESRS E1 climate disclosure.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{TCFD - Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures}\\
\emph{Objective.} Provided a four-pillar framework - governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics \& targets - for climate-related financial disclosure.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The conceptual backbone now carried forward by IFRS S2 and ESRS E1; the standalone task force was disbanded after 2023, so treat it as legacy architecture rather than a live ask.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{IFRS S1 / S2 - ISSB Sustainability \& Climate Disclosure Standards}\\
\emph{Objective.} A global baseline of investor-focused disclosure: S1 (general sustainability-related financial information) and S2 (climate), on a financial-materiality ('outside-in') basis.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The capital-market counterpart to ESRS; most relevant where a seafood firm has an internationally listed parent or investors in adopting jurisdictions.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{SASB Standards - Sustainability Accounting Standards Board Standards}\\
\emph{Objective.} Industry-specific, financially-material sustainability metrics, including an agriculture/protein lens.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Feeds investor metric expectations and now underpins the industry guidance within the ISSB standards rather than operating as a separate ask.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Coller FAIRR - Coller FAIRR Protein Producer Index}\\
\emph{Objective.} Ranks the largest listed animal-protein producers (including aquaculture) on ESG risk factors - feed, antibiotics, welfare, emissions, deforestation - for investor use.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Sector-specific investor scrutiny that a firm is rated by rather than a member of; directly relevant for large/listed producers, largely out of scope for an unlisted 50--250-employee SME.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{DJSI - Dow Jones Sustainability Indices}\\
\emph{Objective.} Selects index constituents via an annual Corporate Sustainability Assessment scoring ESG performance.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Relevant mainly to large listed parents; signals investor-grade ESG performance and is, like FAIRR, a rating-of rather than a membership.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{TNFD - Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures}\\
\emph{Objective.} A framework (the LEAP approach) for assessing and disclosing nature- and biodiversity-related dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The emerging biodiversity/ecosystem lens, highly relevant to marine impacts and dependencies (E4); an EFRAG--TNFD correspondence mapping links it to ESRS.
\paragraph*{1E · Industry / NGO voluntary frameworks}~\\
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GRI - GRI Standards (incl. GRI 13)}\\
\emph{Objective.} The most widely used global sustainability-reporting standards, on an impact ('inside-out') materiality basis; GRI 13 enumerates the topics likely material to the agriculture, aquaculture and fishing sector.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The sector-materiality benchmark used in this thesis's topic-universe cross-check and the single most seafood-relevant existing disclosure map; an EFRAG--GRI interoperability index (Nov 2024) aligns GRI with ESRS.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{UN Global Compact - United Nations Global Compact}\\
\emph{Objective.} Commits signatories to ten principles on human rights, labour, environment and anti-corruption, with an annual Communication on Progress.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A common voluntary commitment and normative reference for governance and social topics; entry-level credibility signal rather than a data standard.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Move to Minus 15 - Move to -15 \textdegree{}C Coalition}\\
\emph{Objective.} A coalition exploring raising the frozen-food storage standard from -18 \textdegree{}C to -15 \textdegree{}C to cut energy use and emissions without compromising safety.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A sector-decarbonisation initiative touching energy (E1) and cold-chain operations; directly relevant to frozen-seafood logistics.
\emph{Status flag.} Governance and the evidence base are still developing; treat membership/standard details as [ASSUMPTION] and verify before citing.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISSF - International Seafood Sustainability Foundation}\\
\emph{Objective.} Promotes science-based tuna conservation and vessel/processor compliance measures, including the ProActive Vessel Register and audited conservation measures.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Tuna-specific; relevant only to canned-tuna processors. Wild-caught scope.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{FIPs / FisheryProgress - Fishery Improvement Projects / FisheryProgress.org}\\
\emph{Objective.} Time-bound, multi-stakeholder projects that move a fishery toward sustainability, with public progress tracking on a central platform; many are engineered toward an eventual MSC assessment.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A sourcing pathway for not-yet-certified fisheries and buyer-recognised improvement evidence - but because FIP requirements largely derive from MSC, it is treated as a non-independent signal (collapsed with MSC) in the convergence count, while its lower strength is reflected by a reduced independence weight. Wild scope.
\emph{Evidence.} FIP effectiveness is contested in the literature: reviews flag stalled progress and weak accountability once market access is gained ('Shifting focus: the impacts of sustainable seafood certification', PLoS ONE). Present FIP participation as intent, not outcome.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{SeaBOS - Seafood Business for Ocean Stewardship}\\
\emph{Objective.} Connects the largest ('keystone') seafood companies with science to drive ocean stewardship on IUU fishing, traceability and climate.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} A large-player, agenda-setting initiative rather than an SME-facing demand; useful as a direction-of-travel reference, aspirational for a 50--250-employee firm.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{RSPO - Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil}\\
\emph{Objective.} Certifies sustainable palm oil along the supply chain via production standards and chain-of-custody models.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Relevant where palm derivatives appear in aquaculture feed or processing inputs; intersects with the EUDR due-diligence obligation.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GDST - Global Dialogue on Seafood Traceability}\\
\emph{Objective.} Defines the key data elements and interoperability formats for digital seafood traceability across wild and farmed supply chains.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The technical spine of the traceability corridor: it lets traceability data flow across IUU/Control-Reg requirements, MSC/ASC chain-of-custody and ESRS value-chain reporting. Buyers may require it, giving it a dual asker/enabler character.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GSSI - Global Sustainable Seafood Initiative}\\
\emph{Objective.} Benchmarks seafood certification schemes against FAO-based criteria so buyers can recognise equivalence between recognised schemes.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Reduces certification fragmentation - it tells buyers which schemes (MSC, ASC, BAP, GLOBALG.A.P.) it recognises rather than demanding data itself; this is why it is treated as a meta-layer and collapsed, not counted as an independent demand.
\emph{Status flag.} Classified as Excluded in the Phase 3 scoring set (Appendix~A.2, Necessity\_Domain\_Matrix\_v8.xlsx) - a benchmark of certification schemes, not an actor that itself demands disclosure from the processor.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{UN SDGs - UN Sustainable Development Goals}\\
\emph{Objective.} Seventeen goals and 169 targets providing a shared global agenda for sustainable development; SDG 14 ('Life below water') is the most directly seafood-relevant.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Used by firms to frame and communicate contribution; a reference frame rather than a data demand, which is why it sits in the enabler layer.
\emph{Status flag.} Classified as Excluded in the Phase 3 scoring set (Appendix~A.2) - an aspirational goal framework with no corporate reporting mechanism or imposed datapoint.
\subsubsection*{Layer 2 - Enablement / methodology layer}
These instruments underpin the demand-side layer. They supply accounting methods, management-system processes, benchmarks or data formats. A firm builds capability on them, but they do not themselves request a disclosure - which is why they are excluded from the demand count yet central to the capability-corridor argument.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{GHG Protocol - Greenhouse Gas Protocol}\\
\emph{Objective.} The dominant corporate GHG accounting and reporting methodology, defining Scopes 1, 2 and 3 and the rules for boundary-setting and calculation.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Underlies every climate disclosure (ESRS E1, CDP, SBTi); it does not itself demand reporting but makes emissions data comparable - a pure capability enabler.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISO 14001 - Environmental Management Systems}\\
\emph{Objective.} Specifies requirements for an environmental management system (EMS) on a plan-do-check-act basis.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Process backbone for environmental data; supports E-topic capability without itself being a disclosure asker.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISO 45001 - Occupational Health \& Safety Management Systems}\\
\emph{Objective.} Specifies requirements for an occupational health \& safety management system.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Underpins own-workforce H\&S (S1) data and processes; the management-system counterpart to the OSH Directive's legal duty.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISO 50001 - Energy Management Systems}\\
\emph{Objective.} Specifies requirements for an energy management system, including energy-performance monitoring.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Supports the quality and auditability of energy-consumption data (ESRS E1).
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISO 14064 - Greenhouse Gas Quantification and Verification}\\
\emph{Objective.} International standard for GHG quantification, reporting and third-party verification at organisation and project level.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Complements the GHG Protocol; commonly referenced in third-party verification of ESRS E1 and CDP climate disclosures.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{LCA methodologies - Life-Cycle Assessment}\\
\emph{Objective.} Methods for quantifying environmental impacts across a product's life cycle.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} Underpins environmental claims made under EmpCo and product-footprint requests from EcoVadis and retail buyers.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{PEF / PEFCR - Product Environmental Footprint (and Category Rules)}\\
\emph{Objective.} The EU's Product Environmental Footprint method and its product-category rules.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The technical basis for substantiating environmental claims under EmpCo.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{ISAE 3000 - Assurance on Non-Financial Information}\\
\emph{Objective.} International assurance standard for non-financial (sustainability) information.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The assurance standard behind CSRD's mandatory limited-assurance requirement and voluntary VSME assurance.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{AA1000 - Stakeholder-Based Assurance}\\
\emph{Objective.} A stakeholder-based assurance and accountability standard.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} An alternative to ISAE 3000's audit-style approach, emphasising stakeholder engagement.
\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Supplier portals / data-exchange platforms}\\
\emph{Objective.} Digital infrastructure through which buyers collect standardised supplier sustainability data.
\emph{In the ecosystem.} The practical channel through which CSRD, CDP and EcoVadis value-chain data requests actually reach the processor.
